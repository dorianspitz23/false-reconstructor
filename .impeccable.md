# Design Context

Scoped to this project. The `.impeccable.md` one directory up belongs to a sibling
project (a language-learning storefront) and does not apply here — its brief is
explicitly anti-scholarly, which is the opposite of what this needs.

### Users
Word people. Conlangers, linguistics students, fantasy and sci-fi writers naming
things, and the large casual audience who enjoyed finding out that `father` and
`pater` are the same word. They arrive from a link, type one silly word, and
either bounce in ten seconds or fall down a two-hour hole. Daylight browsing,
laptop and phone, no urgency, no task to complete — this is play that teaches.

### Brand Personality
**Monastic, meticulous, deadpan.** A scriptorium that takes a joke completely
seriously. The engine applies 126 real sound laws with total rigour to a word
somebody made up thirty seconds ago, and never once winks about it. The humour
is entirely in the commitment. Emotional goal: the delight of being trusted with
something real.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: a leaf of a manuscript. Ruled parchment, iron-gall ink, rubricated
  margins, alternating red-and-blue paraph marks. Scholarly apparatus as the
  actual interface, not as decoration laid on top.
- **Theme**: light. This is a reading surface browsed in daylight, and vellum is
  the whole conceit. A dark version would be a photographic negative of the idea.
- **References**: the Ellesmere Chaucer, Bodleian catalogue scans, the *Oxford
  English Dictionary*'s printed etymology blocks, an Anglo-Saxonist's marked-up
  offprint.
- **Anti-references**: fantasy-tavern medievalism (dragons, faux-aged textures,
  Papyrus), the developer-tool look (monospace everywhere, terminal green),
  generic SaaS light mode (white cards, grey borders, blue buttons).

### Design Principles
1. **The apparatus is the interface.** Rubric, gloss, paraph, manicule and
   bounding line are real scribal tools with real jobs. Each one is used for the
   job it actually did — the marginal date *is* the running head, the manicule
   *is* "the scholarship is unsettled here". Nothing is worn as costume.
2. **Reading down the column is travelling backwards.** The single vertical
   bounding line is the spine of the whole page, and the eye moving down it moves
   from today to 4000 BC.
3. **Gold means unattested.** The asterisk is the linguist's mark for a form
   nobody ever wrote down, and gold leaf was reserved for what mattered most.
   That pun carries the accent colour, and it appears nowhere else.
4. **Three inks, strictly rationed.** Iron gall for text, vermilion for rubric,
   ultramarine for alternation. Ultramarine cost more than gold, so it is used
   about as often.
5. **No monospace.** Phonetic transcription belongs in the scholarly register —
   italic and small caps — not the terminal register. Mono as shorthand for
   "technical" is the tell being designed out.
